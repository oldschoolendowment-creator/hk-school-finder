How schooling works in Hong Kong

Kindergarten K1-K3Age 3 to 6. All privately run. Most join the government's free scheme. You apply direct to each one.
Primary P1-P6Age 6 to 12. Free state places are allocated centrally (Primary One Admission).
Secondary S1-S6Age 12 to 18. Allocated centrally by ability band. Ends with the HKDSE exam, the local equivalent of A-levels.
  • The year starts in September, and the age cut-off is the calendar year. Children born in 2021 start Primary One in September 2027.
  • Your address puts you in a school net, not a catchment around a school. The outlined areas on the map are the five Primary One nets on the island (11, 12, 14, 16 and 18). Distance to a school earns nothing.
  • Primary One has two rounds. In September you apply to one free school in any net, and siblings and points (such as a parent who went there) decide. In January you rank schools in your net, and a computer lottery decides who gets the places that are left.
  • Secondary places go by band. Children are put in one of three bands using their Primary 5 and 6 marks, and the higher bands are allocated first.
  • Most free state schools teach in Cantonese, using traditional Chinese characters. English-medium teaching is mainly at some secondaries and at fee-paying schools.
  • There are no inspection grades or league tables. That's why the pins show the type of school, not how good it is.

What the colours mean

TypeCostHow you get a place
GovernmentFreeCentral allocation by school net (primary) or band (secondary). Run directly by the Education Bureau. Mostly non-religious.
AidedFreeSame central allocation as government schools. Run by a church, charity or other sponsor and paid for by the government, a bit like a voluntary-aided school in England. Many are Christian or Catholic.
Direct Subsidy Scheme (DSS)Fees, varying a lot by schoolApply direct to the school, which may interview. Partly government-funded but sets its own curriculum, language and admissions. Fees are shown in each school's panel.
English Schools Foundation (ESF)High feesEnglish-language schools teaching the IB. Apply through ESF. For primaries, your home address decides which ESF school you're offered, the closest thing here to an English catchment.
InternationalHigh fees, often a debenture or levy tooApply direct. Often British, American, Canadian, French or German curricula. Popular ones have long waiting lists.
Private localFeesApply direct. Independent schools, usually following the local curriculum.
KindergartenFree half-day at scheme kindergartens; fees otherwiseApply direct, usually about a year ahead. Popular ones interview toddlers. Accepting a K1 place needs a registration certificate from the Education Bureau.
Special schoolFreeFor children with special educational needs. Places are arranged through the Education Bureau after an assessment, not through the normal allocation.

School Nets

Hong Kong has no distance-based catchments. Instead, the city is split into school nets. Your address decides your net, and your net decides which government and aided primaries you can list at the "Central Allocation" stage. Within a net, living next door to a school gives no advantage: ties are broken by a computer-generated random number. Click "HK vs England" for the full explanation.
